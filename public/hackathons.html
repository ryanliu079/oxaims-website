Two formats

Start small, then go bigger

Both are planned for the coming year. Dates, venues and partners will be announced.

One day

AI Maker Sprint

One day. One challenge. A working product.

A friendly first hackathon. Small teams take on one focused, real-world challenge, build with AI tools and finish by showing what works.

  • About 6 hours, fitted around a busy Oxford week
  • Teams of 2 to 4
  • One clear challenge with an outcome you can measure
  • Build with Claude Code, Codex and other AI tools
  • Mentors on hand, and AI credits if we can secure them
  • Live demos of working prototypes
  • Any subject, no experience needed

Example challenges

What a challenge could look like

These are examples to show the idea. Real briefs will be published with each event.

A

Software & AI agents

Research automation

The problem
Researchers spend hours pulling out and comparing findings across long papers.
Build
A tool that reads research papers, extracts the key findings and lays them side by side, with references back to the source.
Judged on
It works on papers it hasn’t seen, and every finding traces back to where it came from.
B

Robotics & CAD

Accessible product design

The problem
Everyday objects can be hard to use with limited hand mobility.
Build
Use AI-assisted CAD to design a practical device for one specific accessibility problem.
Judged on
A design that could really be made, backed by a working simulation or a physical prototype.
C

Quantitative & scientific

Intelligent data pipelines

The problem
Analysts lose time cleaning raw data, hunting anomalies and finding what matters.
Build
An automated pipeline that takes an unfamiliar dataset, flags anomalies and writes a useful report.
Judged on
Reproducible results, reliable processing and real insight on data it hasn’t seen.

How projects are judged

We judge what you build, not just what you pitch

Proposed criteria, to be finalised before each event.

  • Functionality & reliability

    35%

    Does it work, every time, on inputs you didn’t prepare for?

  • Usefulness & problem-solving

    30%

    Does it solve a real problem for real people?

  • Technical execution

    20%

    Is it built well, with AI used where it actually helps?

  • Usability & clarity

    15%

    Could someone else pick it up and use it without you?

What this means in practice

  • A working demo counts for more than a polished presentation.
  • Judges will try the core feature themselves.
  • Use AI where it helps, not because it’s there.
  • Quality and real-world impact beat flashy features.
